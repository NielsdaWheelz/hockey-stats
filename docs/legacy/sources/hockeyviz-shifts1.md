## Summary

This is a 2015 data-exploration article about shift mechanics at 5v5 in the 2014-15 season. Its two core findings are (1) **zone-start effects are asymmetric and shorter-lived than commonly assumed** — OZ faceoffs produce a short burst (roughly 15–20 seconds) of elevated shot rates that quickly collapses to average, while NZ and DZ starts are statistically indistinguishable from each other, suggesting breakout success and offensive-zone entry are largely separate skills; (2) **on-the-fly line changes cannot be assigned a rink zone** — puck travel speed is fast enough that the zone before a change is a weak predictor of the zone after it, and vice versa, so OTF shifts are their own creature whose early seconds (30–40 s decay window, longer than faceoffs) determine whether the resulting shift is primarily offensive or defensive. The article establishes the empirical ceiling on what public shift/PBP data can support for zone-start covariate modeling, providing the direct justification McCurdy later records (`hockeyviz-magnus9ev-D17`) for why on-the-fly starts are treated as a single undifferentiated category in Magnus. The article carries **no fatigue analysis**, no rest-based content, and no sequence-based content; its subject is shift STARTS and their effect on within-shift shot-rate trajectories, not fatigue accumulation within or across games.

## Model / method structure sketch

This is a descriptive data analysis, not a fitted model. Two analysis pipelines:

1. **Faceoff shift analysis:** identify shifts starting with a faceoff; assign the shift-start zone (OZ / NZ / DZ — centre-ice and four offside dots pooled; left/right OZ pooled); restrict to 5v5; measure the Corsi shot rate for the home team over seconds 0–60 of the shift; plot smoothed rates by start zone. Faceoff winner not conditioned on.

2. **OTF shift analysis:** identify OTF shifts; look back to the previous PBP event and forward to the next PBP event to assign the "before zone" (D/N/O) and "after zone" (D/N/O); further look back to the previous shift for additional context ("coshifts"); measure Corsi shot rates over a window spanning the change (seconds −30 to +40 or so); plot nine coloured/styled lines for the 3×3 zone-type matrix.

No scoring model, no regression, no parameter estimates — purely observed rates on segmented time windows.

**What is NOT in the article:** stints (the 5v5-no-substitution passages McCurdy uses as Magnus design-matrix rows — distinct from shifts); any fatigue measure; any rest measure (back-to-back rest, cumulative TOI); any within-game fatigue signal; any SequenceFatigue concept; any data-quality issues with the NHL shift feed.

## Evaluation protocol and reported results

No evaluation protocol. Reported results are observed descriptive patterns:
- 2014-15: ≈1 million shifts; 57% OTF; most common weighted length 53 s (F and D identical).
- OZ faceoff burst: ≈3× league average, converging to typical in ~15–20 s.
- NZ and DZ faceoff curves: visually indistinguishable from each other throughout the full 60-second window.
- OTF: 60% involve NZ obviously; shot-rate grouping by before-zone to left, after-zone to right; 30–40 s convergence forward.
- No home/away difference; no period effect.

No calibration, no AUC, no train/test split; these are not model outputs. Figures reference two images (shift-length histogram; OTF coshift-by-nine line plot) that are unpublished to text.
