## judgment after v1

the project can now show a skater or goalie, the underlying observations, an experimental estimate, a comparison group and the components that produce the headline on one publication. jfresh's projected war, hockeyviz's dated ability and our season-level common-workload index answer different player questions. when a rank reversal matters, trace it to a component, opportunity or time horizon where the evidence permits. a persistent disagreement between similarly deployed players is worth investigating. accuracy and individual resolution need evidence for the intended use. [delivery evidence](application-v1-20/verification.md) · [ranking protocol](model-direction.md#comparing-skater-rankings)

a goalie's actual saving can change sign when misses enter the shot population. the csv needs a reliable row filter, and the reader needs both denominators beside the standardized stopping estimate. judge the scientific claim by how much goalie identity helps predict goals and by the size of the difference the evidence can distinguish for a particular tandem. [saros example](v1-review.md#one-goalie-three-questions) · [goalie assessment](../issues/production-model-assessment.md#what-knowing-the-goalie-adds)

on 2026-11-24 hockeyviz had a 2026–27 auston matthews season page. the same player-season on our live api returned `SeasonNotFound`. preserving two dated source selections lets the reader see a player’s first supported game and lets the operator reproduce what the site showed before a correction. fixing the prediction benchmark at the first assigned capture gives later games a second use while the team builds. the first new model can use completed seasons to show how a skater’s 5v5 creation and suppression varied within the year. [matthews on hockeyviz](https://hockeyviz.com/player/matthau97/TOR/2627) · [delivery priorities](../plan.md#next-full-spec-queue) · [dated ability](model-direction.md#retrospective-player-ability)

give the descriptive card one reciprocal teammate comparison. requiring both skaters to dress for the same club removes nearly 278 minutes from mcdavid's 2025–26 “without draisaitl” row. label the game selection on the chart. this lets the reader inspect what happened when the coach split a pair and how often he did so. the remaining linemates, matchups and situations can still explain the difference. [edmonton check](v1-review.md#one-pair-two-game-selections) · [product decision](../product-inventory.md#v2-reciprocal-teammate-view)

use discovery to find comparisons that a ranking hides. anderson and evans have the same rounded net 5v5 impact. their chances against fall on opposite sides of the model's neutral reference. stenberg shows why an extreme should open its evidence even after a skater qualifies on minutes. both questions can be investigated on the published seasons with the planned compact reader. [comparison and support](v1-review.md#what-two-axes-reveal) · [v2 discovery](../product-inventory.md#v2-many-player-scatters)

matthews’s recorded shooting rate fell while his rank held. carlo’s rank fell while his estimated value held nearly steady. give each season its own eligible peers so a reader can inspect both changes. first use the three displayed seasons. reaching careers that ended before 2023–24 takes a separate investment in older facts, estimates and references. [checked histories](v1-review.md#when-value-and-standing-diverge) · [historical depth](../issues/experimental-visual-coverage.md#earlier-season-coverage)

the council recommends three [v2 tracks](../roadmap.md). give readers smaller league responses and clearer cards. give the operator a cheaper path from correction to publication. compare the actual recipes with simple alternatives on matched support and investigate consequential player extremes. for microstats, use the setting and territory estimates already available. if one new observation is worth collecting first, a named passer linked to a shot supports both pass counts and valuation of the chances created. [card decision](../product-inventory.md#v2-microstat-page) · [tracking priority](../issues/microstat-inputs.md#first-tracking-study)

three judgments survived adversarial review:

- start with the cohort fields the view uses. caching may help after measurement, but caching today's whole league objects would retain their parsing and memory cost.
- use file content and coherent open/read identity to frame the next admission change. the successful exact-path preview is documented. the evidence does not identify the metadata writer or establish that another storage move would resolve the problem.
- judge maps and exports at reading width. full-rink maps and material caveats are deliberate choices. move repeated wording to detail and round display values without altering analytical evidence.

the evidence and competing remedies are in the [review](v1-review.md#findings-that-change-what-we-build). these are planning judgments. each change needs its own target and acceptance.
