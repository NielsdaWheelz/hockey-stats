# model direction

the released cards estimate a player's underlying level across a selected season. v2 adds an estimate of how that level varied within the season. observed results describe actual usage and outcomes. a forecast would need a prediction date and later assessment. [product brief](../brief.md) · [v1 review](v1-review.md)

## season-to-date estimates

the first current-season release counts what happened in games through the selected cutoff. valuing those shots under a fixed older execution model would measure the chances faced or created on that scale. such a scorer needs separate identities for its training and scoring games and a rule for previously unseen players and coaches. the existing scorer binds the scored season and inputs to its fitted state. [execution scorer](../../analysis/src/hockey_stats/execution.py#L447)

the modeled follow-up estimates one level across the season played so far. each cutoff starts from the same checked prior-season history. for effects with annual borrowing, the ledger retains the last raw coefficient supported by observations, possibly several seasons earlier. apply the family's role, age and gap rules. a player can qualify for carry in one role and entry in another. fit each eligible current game once. using october's fitted estimate as a new prior and replaying october's games would count them twice. an earlier in-season solution may initialize the optimizer for the same objective. ## retrospective player ability

the **target date** is when the player had the ability we want to estimate. the **evidence cutoff** is the last date from which games may enter the fit. one published fit can estimate many target dates. games on either side of a target date can inform the estimate, up to the cutoff. pin the source revisions and model version so the curve can be reproduced. another fit through a later cutoff can revise it. recorded events, team membership and deployment keep their actual dates.

start with the skater's modeled effect on 5v5 attempt rates for and against under a common reference, in attempts per 60. estimate a time-varying effect from matched events and ice time. the full specification chooses the time scale, how strongly neighboring dates share evidence, and where exposure supports a dated value. smoothing can separate a sustained change from noisy games, blur a sudden change, or borrow heavily across an absence. the newest date has support on only one side. show exposure around the date and the role of borrowing.

compare with a constant within-season effect on the same evidence and reference. use a small known-truth case with stable and changing players to test recovery and timing. held-out game blocks with later games available assess retrospective reconstruction. a prediction benchmark must exclude all evidence after its prediction cutoff from fitting and tuning. checked experimental curves may still have weak individual resolution. ## what the estimates mean

- **value of an opportunity.** the execution model predicts five mutually exclusive outcomes from four conditional heads. score each joint branch over the declared origin and pre-outcome setter mixture, then aggregate. the fully neutral reference retains shot circumstances and inferred setup while setting shooter, setter, goalie and coaching residuals to their reference levels. averaging probabilities over actual players would give a different reference. recorded block status selects a reconstructed release distribution. its all-attempt value therefore has retrospective information that a forecaster would lack before the shot. [chance contract](../specs/experimental-chance.md) · [execution scoring](../../analysis/src/hockey_stats/execution.py)
- **creation and prevention.** the spatial model uses all matched directed ice time, including intervals with no attempts. it multiplies positive team attempt intensity by a normalized forty-mode location distribution and bounded cell quality learned from neutral execution values. on the same support, expected goal rate cannot exceed attempt rate. the skater contrast inserts one focal skater into one reference slot jointly across volume, shape and quality. the neutral lineup supplies the comparison. negative signed effects are meaningful. a negative physical team intensity was a defect in earlier quadratic models. [positive-rate model](../specs/v1-08-positive-attribution.md) · [historical negative-rate evidence](../issues/attribution-negative-rates.md)
- **individual interpretation.** teammates share deployment, opponents and coaches. adjusting for those circumstances changes whose association the model credits. two skaters who always play together can support a combined prediction even when their separate effects are unidentified. a prior can allocate a difference without adding new observations. annual carry can itself raise year-to-year correlation. conditional exposure-based standard errors, optimizer movement, variation across modeling assumptions and calibrated uncertainty each answer different questions. [working uncertainty](../issues/attribution-working-uncertainty.md)
- **standardized value.** the ledger values ten terms at 1,000 5v5 minutes, 100 pp and 100 pk. separate teams/positions contribute to the roster-depth proxy. the same reference lets us compare players with different real usage, at the cost of hypothetical minutes and prior dependence for specialists. shootiness allocates opportunities. crediting it again as a goal term would double count. the index omits specified interactions and actual deployment. peer projected war and retrospective season contribution use different targets. [ledger definition](../specs/v1-12-value-ledger.md) · [value arithmetic](../../analysis/src/hockey_stats/player_value.py)
- **evidence behind a skill name.** ice won/lost depends on movement interpolated between recorded anchors, inferred shift endings and a common successor-value reference. penalty effects describe team award rates with the player present. each estimate is conditional on its recording and reference assumptions. [territory](territory-v1-11/verification.md) · [penalties](mechanisms-v1-10/verification.md)

## reading goalie estimates

stopping asks how many goals a goalie would prevent relative to a neutral goalie facing the same 100 on-net opportunities. the headline excludes the goalie's miss effect. even-strength and penalty-kill panels apply the same goalie coefficients to different shot mixes. the map distributes the stopping estimate over shot origins using shared geometry. a colored patch cannot identify a personal glove-side weakness. actual saving instead sums neutral-goalie expected goals minus goals allowed on the goalie's own matched shots, retaining the actual offensive context. conditioning on shots reaching the net can change even the sign of that total. [reference model](../specs/v1-07-goalie-continuation.md#reference-panels-stopping-and-six-subskills) · [actual results](v1-review.md#one-goalie-three-questions)

the six subskills have different denominators. the first three measure misses among unblocked attempts, a recorded goalie stoppage among on-net shots, and conversion among on-net shots without that stoppage. a stoppage counts when the next record after a save is a whistle coded `goalie-stopped-after-sog` within two seconds. the other three describe the chance of an immediate same-attacking-team reattempt in the same period within five seconds of a save, its neutral expected-goal value when it occurs, and their joint danger per reference save. an intervening recorded event ends the search. a defender can clear a dangerous loose puck. these events describe the sequence and leave physical rebound control unresolved. the joint danger summarizes the two follow-up models and contributes no extra goals to stopping. ## assessing the published recipes

the reviewed production recipes have real fitted values and checked construction. there is no matched held-out comparison of the complete released execution and positive 5v5 recipes against simple controls, and no calibrated interval for an individual player's value. retrospective use of the focal season is appropriate for the selected target. using that same season to estimate fit quality in sample cannot establish out-of-sample performance. [active designs](../model-features.md#experimental-use) · [assessment issue](../issues/production-model-assessment.md)

a bounded evaluation can answer two questions. on held-out games, do the current designs improve execution prediction and lineup-rate prediction on the same observed information and eligible support? in a known-truth case, can the estimator distinguish an entrant and shared linemates at the resolution claimed? report each answer separately. a gain in aggregate rate prediction provides no direct evidence of individual recovery.

use game-level, time-ordered partitions and a declared information cutoff. fit transforms, priors, actor states, references and control models using the permitted training history. separate predicted observable outcomes from reconstructed block-origin opportunities. pair losses on the same games, identify affected strength/source strata and retain zero-event time. state the uncertainty conditional on the split/fits and select any practical gain or loss budget in advance. inspect which standardized player differences change under the comparison. historical seasons are research-exposed. an untouched predictive claim needs a genuinely later assessment.

three published consequences identify concrete diagnostic cases. short-exposure skaters occupy some all-roster spatial extremes. all 940 final-year ice-won/lost terms are small on the common workload. very large actual-context allocations can cancel while the factual lineup rate stays ordinary. each has its own reproduction, interpretation limit and resolution test. ## comparing skater rankings

the seasonal ledger asks what a skater's estimated level is worth at common minutes. jfresh ranks projected wins above replacement. a shared positive goals-to-wins conversion preserves order. subtracting our shared depth reference also preserves order for complete totals within one season and depth-position group. component choices can reverse it. suppose one defender trails another by two goals before finishing and gains five relative to him through finishing. our ledger puts him three ahead. omitting defender finishing, as jfresh does, leaves him two behind even with identical underlying estimates. [ledger arithmetic](../specs/v1-12-value-ledger.md#exact-accounting) · [peer definitions](player-card-references.md#jfresh-and-the-current-hockey-stats-product)

start with our published 5v5 expected-goal creation and suppression rates and topdownhockey's **single-season** even-strength offense and defense components. pin the season, both releases and their evidence cutoffs. verify signs, included strengths and eligibility. comparing rates requires the minutes for each component's own strength population. with only total ice time, keep the peer season totals labeled as workload-dependent.

rank the common eligible skaters separately by position. report the matched count and exclusions. count agreeing pairs, reversals, exact ties and pairs whose order is unresolved at the published precision. rounding can leave the order unresolved even after converting component wins to rates. retain magnitudes in each model's own units. a top-100 extract or selected cards support findings about those players only.

inspect consequential reversals among established skaters with similar deployment. trace the size of each pair's gap, actual role minutes, prior dependence and temporal weighting. when the available evidence cannot explain a reversal, leave its cause open. predictive skill requires held-out observable outcomes, and resolving individual differences requires separate recovery evidence. ## 04m — test the current family on recorded games

borrowing improved held-out joint regional shape loss on the fixed calendar-spanning 261-game assessment. 04n repeated the comparison for complete recorded unblocked-attempt rates on corrected 5v5 support. the regional paired loss difference against independent fitting was −0.108809 (recorded unblocked attempts/60)². native differences remained unresolved, and some physical cell rates were negative. both studies assessed joint lineup prediction conditional on their fitted models. ## 04o — test context and player consequences

extra within-period score/time curves yielded an unresolved change in regional loss and a higher native inverse-share loss. the observed player changes are point sensitivities. adoption remains undecided. the positive experimental models later changed the likelihood, physical-rate construction, valuation and input designs. they cannot inherit these earlier tests as validation. ## retained alternatives

an alternative origin/contact law tests measurement assumptions. changing only the bandwidth of one kernel misses errors common to that family. pooling wrist/snap labels could improve transport across recording changes but erases a distinction. tip-location correction would introduce another reconstruction, while tip-shooter masking is already active in execution. the historical avoidance-only interaction proposal also remains unselected. [measurement issue](../issues/origin-value-sensitivity.md) · [shot-type drift](../issues/recorded-shot-type-drift.md) · [frozen avoidance proposal](../specs/03j-chance-experiment.md#queued-avoidance-experiment)

targeted regularization, annual aging/entry, finer elapsed context and event-history encoders could change different player judgments. the [feature repertoire](../model-features.md) records what is source-backed, selectable, used and missing. 04a's meaningful recorded-rate contrast was 5% of the relevant league-average rate. 03p's margin retained 99% of predictive gain over a constant forecast. neither specifies an acceptable error for the current goal-value ledger. set a claim-specific budget before assessment.
